## Summary statements use different baselines

AEMO’s 2024 and 2026 Integrated System Plans both project coal retirement under the central Step Change scenario, but their headline percentages use different baselines and should not be compared as if they measured the same fleet.

The 2024 ISP described retirement from the then-current 21 GW fleet:

> In Step Change, the most likely scenario, about 90% of the current 21 gigawatts (GW) of coal capacity would retire by 2034-35, and all before 2040.
>
> — [2024 Integrated System Plan](https://www.aemo.com.au/-/media/files/major-publications/isp/2024/2024-integrated-system-plan-isp.pdf?la=en#page=10)

The 2026 ISP described retirement from the fleet remaining in that edition:

> In the Step Change scenario, the ODP projects that nearly two thirds of the NEM’s remaining coal fleet would retire by 2035 (faster than that announced), with all to retire by 2050.
>
> — [2026 Integrated System Plan](https://www.aemo.com.au/-/media/files/major-publications/isp/2026/2026-integrated-system-plan-isp.pdf?rev=7f5dfd18aa1b4a3aab704c424f75afd3&sc_lang=en#page=76)

The 2026 report separately states that 10 major coal-fired generators had retired since 2012. It does not establish that a large share of the 2024 fleet retired between the two ISP editions.

## Figure 18 provides the aligned comparison

[Figure 18 in the 2026 ISP](https://www.aemo.com.au/-/media/files/major-publications/isp/2026/2026-integrated-system-plan-isp.pdf?rev=7f5dfd18aa1b4a3aab704c424f75afd3&sc_lang=en#page=77) plots the 2024 ISP Step Change trajectory and the 2026 Step Change trajectory on the same capacity and time axes. The 2026 trajectory remains above the 2024 trajectory through much of the 2030s and reaches zero around 2050, whereas the 2024 trajectory reaches zero around 2040.

Both editions project retirement faster than announced schedules. They also discuss operating and fuel costs, maintenance, fuel security, renewable competition, reliability and security needs, and government policy settings. The comparison therefore supports a later projected coal exit in the 2026 Step Change trajectory, but not a policy-only explanation for the difference.

AEMO publishes both reports, along with supporting appendices and consultation material, on its [Integrated System Plan program page](https://www.aemo.com.au/energy-systems/major-publications/integrated-system-plan-isp).
